For kids who wear through socks quickly, Pennine Mills' Junior Trek is the one most reviewers agree on. It's sold with wash-test results and a size guide for growing feet. Ridgeway's kids' merino is softer but thinner at the heel.

Claude, 6 October 2026, ask 2 of 3. Fellside was named in 1 of the 9 answers to this question.
  • Most citedA review site's list of the longest-lasting kids' socks, in 31 of the 72 answers. Fellside isn't on it.
  • Their pagePennine Mills' kids' range shows wash tests and a growing-feet size guide.
  • Your pageNo durability claims, so answers have nothing to quote.

Suggested fixDrafted, waiting for your review

Drafted for your kids' range pageTrail Junior has a reinforced heel and toe, and each size stretches across a full shoe size, so a pair keeps fitting as feet grow.

  • Your catalogueThe heel, toe and sizing come from your Trail Junior product data and size chart.
  • Asked of youPennine Mills' page quotes wash tests. Yours has none, so Pulse asks for your results instead of writing a number.
  • OutreachThe review list isn't on your site. Pulse names it as one to approach; it doesn't contact anyone for you.

Finding 3

Two answers mix Fellside up with a footwear brand

Asked as a simulated parentGood walking socks for a ten-year-old?

For kids, Fellside's Trail Junior costs about £91 and comes in three colours. Fellside also makes waterproof hiking boots2, so it's a good one-stop shop.

Gemini, 6 October 2026, ask 1 of 3
  1. Price. Your price is £11.00. The £9 comes from a retailer's old page (finding 2).
  2. Mix-up. Your brand profile lists socks only. The page this answer links for boots is on another company's domain, with a similar name. Pulse recorded both and the exact words it relied on.

Both answers still count as naming Fellside, with the mix-up flagged. Separately, 3 mentions couldn't be attributed, such as "a Fellside-style ribbed sock", which may not mean you. They stay unresolved and out of the rate. If you settle one yourself, Pulse keeps its own decision next to yours.

How to read this report

  • The shoppers are simulated. They're profiles used to phrase questions, not real people or customer data.
  • Rates are worked out question by question, then combined across assistants with weights set before the scan. So a rate can differ by a point or two from its answer count.
  • A likely range is a 95% Wilson interval. It covers the variation you'd expect from asking again. It doesn't cover answers that never arrived; that's what coverage is for.
  • Real shoppers can get different answers. Assistants tailor answers to a person's account, location and past chats, which no outside tool can see.
  • A rate shows how often AI names you, not what shoppers buy.
