Getting started with Pulse

What happens after you sign up, what to have ready, and which parts are yours to do. Setup takes four steps and usually about ten minutes.

Start free See a sample report

Have these ready

  • Your store address. Pulse reads your public product list, so it can match what AI says to the products you actually sell. If your store doesn't share that list, use a Google Merchant Center feed link or a product export from Shopify instead (CSV, up to 20 MB).
  • What you sell, in shoppers' words. "Running socks", not "performance hosiery". Questions are written around it.
  • 3 to 5 competitors. The brands shoppers compare you with. Without them, Pulse can't show your share of the shelf.
  • Where you mostly sell. Optional. You choose the country and language for each brand.

You don't need admin access to your store, or accounts with the AI assistants. Pulse never changes anything on your store.

Set up in four steps

  1. Your store

    Enter your store address, brand name and what you sell, then import your products. Pulse reports how many it found, updated or skipped. You can skip the import and add products later.

  2. Your shoppers

    Pulse drafts shopper profiles from your store, such as "parents buying for growing kids". They're simulated shoppers, and assumptions until you add evidence, for example a note from your order data. Edit them, preview the questions each would ask, then include 2 to 5. Nothing is measured until you approve it.

  3. Your competitors

    Add 3 to 5 brands that sell similar products. Pulse counts how often AI names them next to, or instead of, you.

  4. Your first scan

    Pulse asks AI assistants your shoppers' questions. It usually takes a few minutes, and you can leave it running in the background.

Read your first results

Start with the overview, then open any result to see the questions, the exact answers and the sources they cited. Every rate comes with three things kept apart:

  • Coverage: how many answers arrived, and why any are missing.
  • Unresolved mentions: mentions Pulse couldn't attribute to you with confidence. They're left out, not counted against you.
  • A likely range: how much the rate could move if the same questions were asked again.

Fix what's missing

When answers recommend someone else, Pulse shows the evidence behind it and suggests a change.

  • Drafts use only your facts. A drafted fix uses product facts you've given Pulse. Anything it can't confirm, such as test results, it asks you for instead of writing.
  • You approve before anything is published. Copy an approved draft into your store, or publish it from Pulse to WordPress, Webflow, Contentful or Sanity, or send it to your own system by webhook, as a draft or live.
  • Other people's pages are outreach. When answers lean on a review site, a retailer or a magazine, Pulse names the page and drafts a message. You send it; Pulse doesn't contact anyone for you.
  • Then measure. Later scans show whether the change made a difference, with likely ranges, so a small rise isn't mistaken for a real one.

Invite colleagues as admins, editors or viewers. Admins and editors can make changes; viewers can read everything. Agencies can invite clients as client viewers. How many people you can invite depends on your plan.

Optional, later

  • Publishing connections. Connect WordPress, Webflow, Contentful, Sanity or a webhook to publish approved fixes from Pulse.
  • Shopping agents. From Professional, Pulse gives buying tasks to AI shopping agents and shows whether they find, shortlist and pick you.
  • Your own AI provider keys. Pulse asks the assistants for you. If you'd rather scans run on your own accounts, add your keys in Settings. They're encrypted and used only for your workspace.
  • Your own analytics. On Business, Pulse can bring in visits from AI assistants, so mentions sit next to traffic. Mentions alone aren't sales.
